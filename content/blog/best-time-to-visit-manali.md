Ask ten travellers when to visit Manali and you'll get ten different answers — because Manali genuinely has four or five different seasons, each offering something distinct. Here's the honest month-by-month breakdown we give our own customers.

**December – February: the snow season.** Manali at its most famous. Solang Valley and the Atal Tunnel route hold serious snow, and the town takes on the postcard look most visitors come for. It's also the busiest window around Christmas and New Year — book 3–4 weeks ahead, and expect hotel prices at their annual peak.

**March – June: the classic family season.** The snow retreats to the high ridgelines, days sit at a perfect 15–25°C, and every road — including Rohtang-adjacent routes — is open. This is when schools break for summer, so plan early for May–June travel.

**July – August: monsoon, with caveats.** The valley turns impossibly green and rates drop, but landslides and road closures are real. We plan monsoon itineraries with built-in buffer days and alternatives, but first-time Himalayan travellers usually enjoy another season more.

**September – October: the connoisseur's window.** Post-monsoon air is at its clearest — this is when you actually see the big peaks, not just clouds where peaks should be. Crowds thin, prices soften, and Dussehra week in the Kullu valley is a genuine cultural spectacle.

**November: quiet and pretty.** A shoulder month with thin crowds, cold-but-manageable days, and early snow dustings on the higher ridges. Great for couples who want the mountains mostly to themselves.

The short version: families should target March–June, snow-seekers December–February, photographers September–October, and budget travellers the shoulder weeks. And whenever you go — message us.
